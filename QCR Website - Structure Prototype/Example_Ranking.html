Page Quality Comparison

Picture quality is an x-factor. It is shown alongside the grade, but it does not count toward the 100-point total.

How To Compare Against Raws

  1. Open the raw page and the evaluated page side by side.
  2. Check that every page and panel exists in the translated release.
  3. Compare cropping, compression, grain, blurring, contrast, cleaning, redraws, and visible text replacement.
  4. Look for missing panels or pages before judging normal image clarity; missing content can affect readability and picture quality.
  5. Compare against a standard-candle release to decide whether the image sits closer to Very High, Average, Poor, or Unreadable.

Criterion Walkthrough

Each scored criterion should have a grade, a point value, and notes explaining the evidence behind it.

Readability 0-40p

Readability asks how easy the translation is to understand during normal reading. This should be judged before over-correcting the text in your head. If a release looks readable at first but collapses when compared to competent translations, that belongs in the notes.

  • Compare against Webtoon for effortless readability.
  • Compare against Valir Scans when the text is technically readable but context is often obscured.
  • Compare against Nyx Scans when the release looks readable but falls apart under closer comparison.
  • Record missing pages or panels here if they interrupt normal reading.

Consistency 0-20p

Consistency measures stability across chapters, series, and time. It is stricter than most categories because the framework treats stability as something that either holds or does not hold.

  • Consistent: no large swings in quality.
  • Inconsistent: quality swings with no clear pattern.
  • Consistently-Inconsistent: quality swings with a repeatable pattern.
  • Series-Dependent / Genre-Dependent: the pattern source is specifically identified.

Translation Quality 0-30p

Translation quality judges wording, grammar, phrasing, and meaning directly. The S and A tiers are strict because tiny wording differences matter more at high quality levels. The C and B tiers are where most readable translations settle.

  • Use raws or a trusted comparison translation to check meaning when possible.
  • Look for incorrect pronouns, broken grammar, nonsensical phrasing, and mistranslated concepts.
  • Separate direct translation quality from readability when a line is understandable but still wrong or awkward.

Localization Quality 0-10p

Localization is intentionally more flexible because it changes by language, region, and audience. It should still be scored when awkward localization breaks context or makes the release feel unnatural.

  • Check whether names, pronouns, idioms, cultural references, and tone remain understandable.
  • Record cases where grammar or wording erases context.
  • Use the looser 10-point spread because localization has more subjective judgment than the other categories.

Uncertainty Rules

If a grade is uncertain, use only broad uncertain grades: S?, A?, B?, C?, D?, or F?. Do not use A+? or A-?. Uncertain grades are excluded from the overall grade until uncertainty is resolved.

Final Notes Structure

Each ranking should end with notes by criterion. The note should explain the evidence, the comparison point, and any reason the grade should be revisited later.
